## The Core Truth

Every second counts. Viewers decide in 3 seconds whether to keep watching. A short that holds attention for 30 seconds with zero drop-off will be pushed by the algorithm. One that loses people at second 10 will die at 2K views. Your job is to make every single second earn the next one.

---

## The 5-Part Script Formula

Every script follows this exact skeleton. No exceptions.

### 1. HOOK (Sentence 1) — 3-5 seconds
The most important part of the entire video. Must be:
- **Shocking** — a surprising fact, bold claim, or emotional trigger
- **Specific** — concrete details, not vague statements
- **Information gap** — creates a question in the viewer's mind they NEED answered

✅ Good: "MrBeast pays his janitor over a million dollars a year."
✅ Good: "This woman got a security guard fired for doing his job."
❌ Bad: "Here's something crazy about MrBeast."
❌ Bad: "You won't believe what happened next."

**Why it works:** The hook creates cognitive tension — the viewer's brain needs resolution. They can't scroll away without knowing why.

### 2. SUPPORTING HOOK (Sentence 2)
Subvert the viewer's first assumption. Whatever they think the answer is — tell them they're wrong.

✅ Good: "You may think it's because he won some sort of challenge, but this couldn't be more wrong."
❌ Bad: "Let me tell you the full story."

**Why it works:** The viewer just formed a hypothesis. You destroyed it. Now they MUST keep watching to form a new one.

### 3. DEVELOPING IDEA #1 (Sentences 3-4)
Set the scene. Fast timeline, minimal details, no payoff yet.

- Open with a fast date/timeline: "Back in 2018" NOT "On the 12th of December 2018"
- Give 1-2 sentences of context that build the story
- "Edge them but don't make them cum" — build tension toward the reveal without giving it away

✅ Good: "Back in 2018, MrBeast hired this janitor to clean up crazy messes they would make from videos. The janitor was very shy, even scared to talk to MrBeast himself."
❌ Bad: Long backstory with unnecessary details about where MrBeast lives, what year he started YouTube, etc.

### 4. DEVELOPING IDEA #2 + SETUP PAYOFF (Sentences 5-6)
Edge the viewer more. Narrow the possibilities. Ask a question.

- These sentences build toward what you're about to reveal
- The viewer should start to SENSE the payoff is coming
- End with a question that the viewer is already thinking

✅ Good: "Comments kept asking for the janitor to be in more videos, and he eventually became part of the MrBeast crew. So, who is it?"
❌ Bad: Flat statements that don't build anticipation.

### 5. PAYOFF + SUBSCRIBE INCENTIVE (Sentences 7-8)
Reveal + CTA. Short, clear, satisfying.

- The reveal must make them go "damn wtf"
- Don't overexplain — one sentence max
- Subscribe CTA IMMEDIATELY after payoff
- CTA must relate to THIS specific story — never generic

✅ Good: "Well it's actually Chandler. Subscribe if he's your favorite."
✅ Good: "Subscribe if you think she was wrong."
❌ Bad: "Subscribe for more drama content."
❌ Bad: "Don't forget to like and subscribe."

**CTA impact:** Even a bad contextual CTA = 3x more subs. A good one = 5-7x more subs. NEVER skip it.

## Script Quality Rules

### Length
- **Exactly 7-8 spoken lines**
- **30-35 seconds** of narration (~85-100 words at 2.8 words/sec)
- Not shorter (not enough story). Not longer (attention drops).

### Flow
- Read it aloud. Does it sound natural?
- No repeated words/names — use pronouns, synonyms
- Each sentence must flow into the next with no awkward transitions
- If there's a natural pause where someone might scroll away — tighten it

### The Fluff Test
For every sentence, ask: "If I remove this, does the story still work?"
- If yes → it's fluff. Delete it.
- If no → it stays.
Every word must either advance the story or build tension. Zero exceptions.

### Threading
The middle section (sentences 3-6) must carry the story smoothly from hook to payoff. Think of it as a thread — one pull should lead naturally to the next. If there's a logical gap or the story jumps, the viewer's brain disengages.

## Scouting Principles

### Proof-of-Concept First
Don't guess what might go viral. Find stories that are **already proven viral** on other platforms (TikTok, Instagram, Twitter/X). Then repackage with your own narration and angle.

**Why:** Starting with content that already has millions of views reduces risk to near-zero. The story is proven — you just need to tell it better.

### Source Hierarchy
1. **Stories with existing viral clips** (video evidence) — best
2. **Stories trending with high engagement** across multiple platforms — good
3. **Breaking news with drama angle** — riskier but can be first-mover advantage
4. **Raw trending topics with no clip evidence** — worst, avoid unless extraordinary

### What Makes a Good Drama Story
- **Conflict** — someone vs someone, betrayal, unexpected reveal
- **Emotional trigger** — anger, shock, empathy, disbelief
- **Relatability** — viewer can see themselves or someone they know in the situation
- **Clip availability** — if there's no visual to pair with it, it's much harder to execute

### Red Flags in Scouting
- All seeds from the same source (monoculture risk)
- Lifestyle/wholesome content disguised as drama (weak conflict)
- Stories that need too much context to understand (won't fit 30 seconds)
- Old stories with no new angle

## Title Formula

**Format: Short + Emotional + Emoji + Question-based**

✅ "She made the security guard lose his job 😳"
✅ "His janitor makes more than most CEOs 🤯"
✅ "Would you forgive your best friend for this? 😬"
❌ "Celebrity Drama Update March 4th"
❌ "BREAKING: New drama in the rap community #drama #shorts"

- Titles don't drive Shorts discovery (the algorithm does), but they drive **curiosity and comments**
- Questions in titles increase comment activity, which signals engagement to YouTube
- One emoji max. Pick one that adds emotional tone.
- Keep it under 50 characters if possible.

## Posting Strategy

- **Best time:** 12-3 PM EST (peak US + European audience activity)
- **Frequency:** 1/day to start. Scale to 2-4/day only after consistently hitting 10-20K views.
- **Never exceed 5/day** — triggers spam detection
- **Pre-schedule 5-10 days ahead** to avoid daily scramble
- **Hashtags don't matter** for Shorts. Skip them.
- **Descriptions are irrelevant** for Shorts. Blank or simple "subscribe" is fine.
- **Never mark "Made for Kids"** — kills reach entirely
- **Leave category as "People & Blogs"** — changing it does nothing

## Retention: The Only Metric That Matters

### Target: 90%+ retention, flat graph

- **Flat graph** = viewers staying engaged throughout = algorithm pushes further
- **Declining graph** = story lost momentum = video dies at test audience stage
- **Small dip at start** is normal (viewers previewing). Big dip at start = weak hook.
- **Mid-video dip** = threading lost momentum or a fluff sentence killed pacing
- **End drop-off** = payoff wasn't satisfying or came too late

### YouTube's Test Process
1. YouTube shows your Short to a small test audience (~few hundred to 10K)
2. If retention + swipe-away rate are strong, it pushes to larger audience
3. This repeats in waves — each wave is bigger if metrics hold
4. Videos with 90%+ retention and strong swipe rate can reach millions

### Key Metrics
- **Retention %** — how much of the video viewers watch (aim 90%+)
- **Swipe rate** — how many people stop scrolling to watch (85%+ is elite)
- **Comments** — engagement signal, boosted by question-based titles/CTAs
- **Shares** — strongest signal of all; content must be share-worthy

## Editing Principles (for future reference)

- **Cut ALL dead air** — no silent gaps between sentences
- **Voiceover pacing is the skeleton** — edit audio first, then add visuals
- **Visual cues in intro** — arrows, circles, highlights to direct attention
- **"Sneak peek" technique** — tease the payoff visually in the first 3 seconds (e.g., blurred figure, circled person)
- **Every frame serves a purpose** — either inform or intrigue
- **Clean clips only** — no watermarks, no low-res, no visual clutter
- **Test without sound** — if the visuals alone hold attention, the clip is strong

## Your QA Checklist (Apply EVERY Run)

Before passing any stage, ask yourself:

### Seeds
- [ ] Would I personally click on this story?
- [ ] Is there genuine conflict/drama, not just lifestyle content?
- [ ] Are sources diverse (not all from one account)?
- [ ] Do most seeds have existing viral clips or high engagement proof?

### Scripts
- [ ] Does the hook make me stop and pay attention?
- [ ] Does the supporting hook destroy my first assumption?
- [ ] Is every sentence earning the next one? (No fluff)
- [ ] Does the payoff land? ("damn wtf" reaction)
- [ ] Is the subscribe CTA natural and story-specific?
- [ ] Read it aloud — does it flow? Any awkward transitions?
- [ ] Is it 7-8 lines / 30-35 seconds?

### Assets
- [ ] Do clips match the story being told?
- [ ] Are they high quality (no watermarks, good resolution)?
- [ ] Do they work visually without sound?
- [ ] Is there emotional content in the visuals?

### Final Check
- [ ] Would I be proud to upload this?
- [ ] If I saw this in my Shorts feed, would I watch the whole thing?
- [ ] If not — iterate. Don't ship mediocre work.
